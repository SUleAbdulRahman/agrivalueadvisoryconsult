Sector Notes: rice after the 2027 Budget

What changes for Ghanaian rice if the budget measures go ahead, and what buyers will ask of suppliers.

AgriValue Advisory ConsultDecember 2026 edition3 min read

The most significant market signal in the 2027 Budget is for rice. If the announced measures are implemented, the buyers of Ghanaian rice, and what they require, will change.

What was announced

  • From 2027, rice import licences are to be linked to purchases from verified Ghanaian farmers and millers, with import quotas reduced gradually.
  • A target to end commercial rice imports by December 2030.
  • Procurement rules to be amended so that schools, hospitals and public institutions buy local rice and staples.
  • Rice development hubs across the Volta Basin, Western Ghana, Northern Ghana and other flood plains, with a target of an additional 100,000 hectares under rice.

Who the new buyers could be

  • Importers who need local purchases to keep their licences.
  • Public institutions and the suppliers who serve them.
  • Millers who need steady paddy supply to meet that demand.

What they are likely to ask for

  • Proof of origin and volume: who grew it, where, and how much you can supply each month.
  • Consistent quality: moisture, broken grains and foreign matter within agreed limits, batch after batch.
  • Packaging and delivery: agreed bag sizes, labelling and delivery schedules.
  • Paperwork: registration, invoices and receipts, because licence and procurement rules will need evidence.
Not yet in force

These are budget announcements. The rules for who counts as "verified", and the amended procurement rules, have not been published. Prepare, but do not invest heavily on the assumption of guaranteed demand.

One step this month

If you grow, aggregate or mill rice, write down your last season's volume, your quality checks and your current buyers on one page. That is the starting point for any verification process.
