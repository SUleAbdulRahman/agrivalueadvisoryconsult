Systems Guidance: stop small cash payments leaking

The imprest petty cash system, explained end to end, and how to install it in one week.

AgriValue Advisory ConsultDecember 2026 edition3 min read

Small cash payments are where many agribusinesses quietly lose money: transport for a delivery, a padlock for the store, airtime for the field officer. Each is small. Together, over a season, they add up, and nobody can say where it went. This month's improvement is one of the simplest controls in finance: the imprest petty cash system.

How it works

  • Set a fixed float. Decide the amount the petty cash box holds, for example enough for two weeks of small payments. Write it down. This is the imprest amount.
  • Name one custodian. One person holds the box and the voucher book. Not three.
  • No payment without a voucher. Every payment gets a numbered voucher: date, amount, purpose, who received the money, and who approved it. Attach the receipt where there is one.
  • Set a limit. Payments above a set amount do not go through petty cash. They go through the bank.
  • Top up only against vouchers. When the box runs low, count it. Cash in the box plus the vouchers must equal the fixed float. Top up by exactly the total of the vouchers, no more.
The one check that matters

Cash in the box + value of vouchers = the fixed float. If it does not balance, you know at once, while the amounts are small and the memory is fresh.

Install it in one week

  1. 01
    Day 1: decide the float and the limitLook back at last month's small payments to set a realistic amount.
  2. 02
    Day 2: buy a numbered voucher book and a lockable boxNumbered vouchers make missing pages obvious.
  3. 03
    Day 3: appoint the custodian and the approverWrite both names down. The approver should not be the custodian.
  4. 04
    Day 4 onwards: run itFrom now on, no voucher, no cash.
  5. 05
    Every top-up: count and signThe custodian and approver count together, sign the count, and file the vouchers by month.

What it gives you

A paper trail for every small payment, a monthly total you can put into your accounts, and evidence of financial control. Funders and lenders look for exactly that when they assess whether an organisation can manage money.
