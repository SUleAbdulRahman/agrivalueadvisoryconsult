What Is a Funding-Ready Pack, and Does Your Agribusiness Need One?

Most applications are not lost on the idea. They are lost on missing documents, numbers that cannot be verified, and no counterpart contribution. Here is what being ready actually means, and how to check where you stand.

AgriValue Advisory ConsultNovember 20267 min read

Many funding calls give applicants a limited window, and some come around only once a year. The FSRP matching grant scheme, for example, plans yearly calls for proposals. Organisations that win are rarely the ones with the best idea on the day a call opens. They are the ones whose documents, numbers and partners were already in order.

What is a Funding-Ready Pack?

A Funding-Ready Pack is one organised, checked set of the documents, figures and decisions a funder will ask for, prepared before a call opens rather than during it. It is not a proposal for one particular grant. It is the foundation every proposal is built on.

Our Funding-Ready Pack™ service builds that foundation, screens your eligibility against specific calls, and then adapts the pack into a submission: narrative, budget, results framework and supporting annexes.

What goes into a pack

  • Registration, ownership and tax records — proof the business is legally registered, who owns it, and that its tax affairs are in order. Ownership evidence matters: the FSRP scheme is open to Ghanaian-owned registered companies, and the GEA SME programme required majority Ghanaian shareholding of 51% or more.
  • A bank account in the organisation's name — not a personal account.
  • Financial statements and management accounts, with a cash-flow forecast for the project period.
  • Governance records — constitution or regulations, current officers, and who is authorised to sign.
  • A project concept and results framework — the problem, activities, outputs, outcomes, indicators and who benefits.
  • A costed budget and funding plan, including your own contribution.
  • Evidence of track record and market links — buyer letters, supply agreements, farmer-group records, past project reports.
  • Policies and controls — safeguarding, anti-fraud, conflict of interest, procurement and data protection, where the funder asks for them.
  • Scheme-specific registrations — for example, FSRP requires small agribusinesses, SMEs, FBOs and productive alliances to be registered on the Ghana Agriculture and Agribusiness Platform (GhAAP).

Five signs your organisation is not yet ready to apply

  1. 01
    You cannot produce your core documents within a dayRegistration, ownership, tax records and bank details should sit in one folder, current and consistent with each other. If assembling them takes weeks, you will miss windows.
  2. 02
    Your numbers cannot be verifiedFunders commonly run due diligence on shortlisted applicants; GEA's application guidelines say so for its SME grant programme. Figures in your proposal must match your accounts and bank statements. No separate business account and no written accounts is a red flag.
  3. 03
    You cannot explain what the money buys"We need funds to expand" is not a project. Funders look for specific activities, outputs, results, indicators and a clear statement of who benefits.
  4. 04
    You have no counterpart contributionMatching grants expect you to put in your own resources. The FSRP matching grants manual refers to evidence of matching funds. If you cannot show your contribution, you are not ready. Confirm the exact percentage in each call.
  5. 05
    You have not checked eligibility against the call itselfPaperwork does not create eligibility. Ownership, legal status, scheme registration, geography and value-chain limits still apply. FSRP's two windows, for instance, target different applicants and have different ceilings.
A file existing is not the same as a file being ready

Track every document as Ready, Needs review or Missing. Only mark it Ready once you have checked that the content is accurate, current and relevant to the specific call. Funders do not share one universal document list, so always work from the call's own requirements.

Does your agribusiness need one?

You need the readiness. You do not necessarily need to pay someone for it.

You can probably build it yourself if

You already keep proper accounts, hold your registration and ownership documents, and have a defined project. The checklist above, plus a few weeks of tidying, may be enough.

A pack is worth paying for if

  • Two or more of the five signs above describe you.
  • You plan to apply to several funders and want one reusable base.
  • A call window is short and you need to move fast.
  • You have been rejected before without clear reasons.

A pack will not help if

You are simply not eligible: wrong ownership structure, wrong value chain, or outside the scheme's target area. No amount of preparation fixes that. This is why eligibility screening comes first, before any money is spent on writing.

Where a ready organisation can aim today

Funding programmes change, so confirm what is actually open before you plan around it. Three reference points:

  • FSRP Matching Grant Scheme (Ministry of Food and Agriculture, World Bank-financed). Window 1 covers production-stage activities, with a ceiling of US$150,000. Window 2 covers post-production firms (storage, drying, processing, haulage), with a ceiling of US$500,000. Calls are planned yearly. Confirm the current call, counterpart percentage and deadlines with FSRP or MoFA.
  • GEA SME grant programme. At its 2023 launch, grant caps were US$50,000 for small and US$70,000 for medium enterprises, with priority for women-owned, youth-owned and green businesses. Check gea.gov.gh for the current status.
  • AfDB Agriculture Fast Track Fund — for larger infrastructure projects. See our guide: AfDB Agriculture Fast Track Fund: what Ghanaian agribusinesses need to know.
Check the funder is still there

Programmes can end earlier than planned. ACDI/VOCA reported in July 2025 that the USAID-funded Ghana Market Systems and Resilience Activity ended after about two and a half years of a planned five. Before you spend time or money on an application, confirm that the call is open and the funder is still active.

How to start this week

  1. 01
    List every document and mark its statusReady, Needs review or Missing. Give each one an owner and a review date.
  2. 02
    Fix the one gap most likely to delay youUsually a missing tax record, an out-of-date registration, or accounts that were never written up.
  3. 03
    Read a real call's guidelines line by lineCheck your eligibility against them. Do not assume.
  4. 04
    Confirm your own contribution in writingCash, a bank facility or in-kind support, as the call allows.
  5. 05
    Review the pack every quarterUpdate it when anything changes, and before each submission.
