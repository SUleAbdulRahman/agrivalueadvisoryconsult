After USAID: Where Northern Ghana Agribusinesses Can Look for Funding Now

USAID's Feed the Future programme in Ghana ended early, and no replacement of the same size has appeared. Here is what happened, the funding routes that exist today, and which ones to stop planning around.

AgriValue Advisory ConsultNovember 20267 min read

If your business, cooperative or NGO in Northern Ghana was counting on USAID-funded grants, training or subsidised inputs, you are not alone. The region was the largest beneficiary of USAID funding in Ghana, at more than US$200 million, according to the executive director of the civil society group NORSAAC. This guide sets out what happened, which funding routes exist today, how reliable each one looks, and how to decide where to spend your time.

What happened to USAID funding in Ghana?

US foreign aid was suspended in early 2025. The US Government Accountability Office reported in September 2026 that USAID terminated about 6,780 of its 7,510 awards between January 2025 and March 2026, and that the State Department announced USAID stopped administering awards as of 1 July 2025.

For Northern Ghana's agribusinesses, the key programme was the Feed the Future Ghana Market Systems and Resilience (MSR) Activity, a US$39.5 million effort led by ACDI/VOCA. In July 2025 ACDI/VOCA reported that the project ended earlier than planned, about halfway through its five-year plan.

There is no USAID window to apply to

If anyone offers to help you apply for USAID or MSR sub-grants, ask for written proof that a call is open. We found no reporting of a replacement programme of comparable size. The routes below are smaller, different in kind, and mostly work through banks or ministries rather than direct grants.

Funding routes to consider now

1. The northern food-security revolving fund (loans through rural banks)

In April 2025 the Ministry of Food and Agriculture and ARB Apex Bank signed an agreement to manage a US$1 million revolving fund. It sits within the Food and Nutrition Security Resilience Project, which is financed by a US$20 million grant from the Global Agriculture and Food Security Programme (GAFSP), with technical support from the African Development Bank.

  • Who it targets: smallholder farmers, with priority for women-led groups growing vegetables, groundnut, cowpea and soybean. Microenterprises and agribusinesses can also apply to scale up.
  • Where: named districts in the Northern Savannah zone, including Tamale Metro, Mion, Savelugu, Nanton, West Gonja, Bawku West, Wa Municipal, Sissala East, Nandom, East Mamprusi, Mamprugu-Moagduri and Krachi East.
  • How: loans go through ARB Apex Bank's rural and community banks, at 10 percent interest with a flexible repayment schedule.
  • Caution: this is a loan, not a grant, and the fund is small. Ask your rural bank whether money remains and whether your district is covered.

2. FSRP Matching Grant Scheme (grants, with your own contribution)

The Ministry of Food and Agriculture runs this scheme under the World Bank-financed West Africa Food System Resilience Programme. Window 1 covers production activities with a ceiling of US$150,000. Window 2 covers post-production firms such as processors, with a ceiling of US$500,000. Applicants must be Ghanaian-owned registered companies, and small agribusinesses, SMEs, FBOs and productive alliances must be registered on the Ghana Agriculture and Agribusiness Platform (GhAAP).

Status: calls are planned yearly, but we could not confirm a call that is open today or the exact counterpart percentage. Contact FSRP or the Ministry before you plan around it.

3. Bank credit supported by GIRSAL and Development Bank Ghana

GIRSAL gives guarantees to lenders, and Development Bank Ghana provides long-tenure funds that banks can on-lend. You do not apply to GIRSAL directly; you apply to a bank or other lender, which can then receive guarantee cover. GIRSAL also provides technical support to help farmer groups and agribusinesses become ready to borrow.

Fit: businesses with a bankable plan and records. This is credit you must repay, so your accounts and cash-flow forecast matter more than the strength of your story.

4. The Feed Ghana Programme (government support for farmer groups)

This government programme offers farmer groups access to inputs, financing initiatives and links to processors. To take part, farmers must belong to a farmer-based organisation or cooperative of 15 to 40 members, registered through the district agricultural department.

Reality check: in May 2026 SEND Ghana and partners reported that farmers in the Northern, Oti, Volta and Bono East regions had not received promised inputs even though the rains had started. Do not build your cash flow on inputs that have not arrived.

Routes that look closed or unconfirmed

  • AfDB Agriculture Fast Track Fund. It pays for preparing large projects, not running costs, and we could not confirm an open call. See our guide to the fund.
  • GIZ Agri-Business Facility for Africa. Its Matching Grant Fund call, which opened in July 2024, is shown as closed. Its latest listed competition is aimed at young and emerging agripreneurs, and its deadline was 13 November 2026.
  • IFAD and Ministry matching grants (GASIP). This earlier programme appears to have closed, and we found no active call. Check before you rely on any guide that still lists it.
  • Ghana Enterprises Agency SME grants. We found no open 2026 call. Check gea.gov.gh.

Which route fits which business?

  • Women's groups and smallholders in the listed districts needing working capital: start with the rural bank revolving fund, and register your FBO properly.
  • Cooperatives and FBOs hoping for government support: confirm your registration with the district agricultural department, but plan as if inputs may be late.
  • Processors and aggregators that can co-fund an investment: look at FSRP Window 2 and bank credit backed by GIRSAL.
  • Large infrastructure projects above roughly US$1 million: check the AfDB fund's current status before spending on an application.
Grant, loan or inputs: know which one you are applying for

A loan must be repaid, here at 10 percent for the revolving fund. A matching grant needs your own money in the project. Input support depends on deliveries that have been delayed. Treat each differently in your cash-flow plan.
