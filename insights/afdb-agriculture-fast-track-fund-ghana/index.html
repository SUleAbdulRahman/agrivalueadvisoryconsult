AfDB Agriculture Fast Track Fund: What Ghanaian Agribusinesses Need to Know

The AFT Fund pays for the expensive early work that gets large agriculture projects ready for investment. It is not a general grant. Here is what it funds, who it suits, and what to check before you spend time on it.

AgriValue Advisory ConsultNovember 20266 min read

The African Development Bank's Agriculture Fast Track Fund (AFT) is often described as a source of large grants for African agribusiness. That description skips the important detail: it pays for the expensive early work that gets a big agriculture project ready for investment. It does not pay to build the project or run the business.

The short version

  • AFT is a multi-donor trust fund hosted by the African Development Bank, approved in May 2013, with a stated volume of US$23 million and contributions from the United States and Sweden (per the AfDB's fund page, last updated May 2024).
  • It finances project preparation: the studies and planning that come before construction and lending.
  • Ghana has been an eligible country. In 2019 the AfDB announced 17 new projects across Ghana and seven other African countries.
  • We found no open call and no awards announced after that 2019 round. Confirm the current status with the AfDB before you invest time.
What we could not confirm

Published grant sizes differ by source and period. Calls from 2013 to 2017 described grants of roughly US$100,000 to US$1.5 million for projects with total investment above US$1 million. The AfDB's fund page describes tiers of up to US$500,000 and US$500,000 to US$1 million. We could not find a current call, so treat any figure you read, including these, as a starting point to verify rather than a promise.

What is project preparation finance?

A processing plant, a cold store, an irrigation scheme or a logistics hub usually needs substantial spending before a single brick is laid. Feasibility studies, market research, financial models and environmental and social impact assessments all cost money, and banks and investors typically want them done before they lend.

AFT pays for that early stage so a viable project can reach financing. In past calls it did not cover construction costs, working capital, salaries, training, capacity building or general technical assistance.

Who it was designed for

Earlier calls were open to:

  • Private agribusiness firms, including SMEs
  • Government agencies preparing agriculture infrastructure projects
  • Farmer organisations and non-governmental organisations
  • Fund managers with clients seeking last-mile financing

Past calls also required the overall project investment to be above US$1 million, and the preparation work to be completed within 24 months of signing the grant agreement.

How applications worked in earlier calls

Applicants first completed an online eligibility screening. Only those who passed were invited to submit a concept note, which in the calls we reviewed had to be sent within two weeks. Later stages followed for shortlisted projects. Each call set its own country list, so always read the current guidelines.

Is it realistic for your business?

  1. 01
    Is the project large enough?Past calls expected total investment above US$1 million. Most small farms and early-stage processors are well below that.
  2. 02
    Is it bankable agriculture infrastructure?The fund targets projects that can attract investment once prepared: storage, processing, irrigation, logistics, market infrastructure.
  3. 03
    Do you have a credible sponsor?A track record, the ability to fund the rest of the project, and identified buyers or off-takers.
  4. 04
    Can you finish preparation in 24 months?With a clear scope, a team and a timetable for the studies.
  5. 05
    Is a call actually open?Check the AfDB fund page and your nearest AfDB country office before you start any application work.

If AFT is not the right fit

  • FSRP Matching Grant Scheme, Window 2 (Ministry of Food and Agriculture, World Bank-financed): up to US$500,000 for post-production firms such as processors and aggregators with strong smallholder links, with calls planned yearly. Confirm the current call with FSRP or MoFA.
  • Smaller SME programmes such as the Ghana Enterprises Agency's SME grants, which have smaller caps and a Ghanaian-ownership requirement.
  • Get ready first. Whatever you choose, a funder will check your documents and figures. See What is a Funding-Ready Pack, and does your agribusiness need one?
A caution on "guaranteed" grants

Be wary of anyone who charges a fee and promises guaranteed access to a development-bank grant. Calls are competitive, application portals are run by the funder, and eligibility screening is the funder's decision. Use official AfDB channels to confirm whether a call is open.
