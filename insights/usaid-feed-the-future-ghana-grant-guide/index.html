USAID Feed the Future Ghana: Current Status and Where to Look Instead

USAID's Feed the Future programme in Ghana was suspended in 2025 and has not been reinstated. Here is what actually happened, and the funding routes Ghanaian agribusinesses can look at in 2026, including which ones we could confirm are open.

AgriValue Advisory ConsultUpdated November 20266 min read
Important update — July 2026

This article previously described the USAID Feed the Future Market Systems and Resilience (MSR) Activity as an active grant window. It is not. USAID funding to Ghana, including MSR, was suspended in January 2025 and has not been restored. We have rewritten this article to reflect the current situation accurately, and it was updated again in November 2026 to separate the routes we could confirm from those we could not.

What happened to USAID Feed the Future in Ghana?

In January 2025, the United States government abruptly suspended USAID funding to Ghana and dozens of other countries. The US Government Accountability Office reported in September 2026 that USAID terminated about 6,780 of its 7,510 awards between January 2025 and March 2026, and that the State Department announced USAID stopped administering foreign assistance awards as of 1 July 2025 (GAO report GAO-26-108607).

The Feed the Future Ghana Market Systems and Resilience (MSR) Activity — a five-year programme implemented by ACDI/VOCA to support smallholder farmers and agribusinesses across Northern Ghana — lost its funding pipeline along with the rest of USAID's Ghana portfolio.

Is there any sign of it coming back?

Not as of the most recent reporting. There is no indication from USAID or the Ghanaian government that MSR or the broader Feed the Future portfolio will be reinstated.

Practical implication for agribusinesses

If you run an agribusiness, cooperative, or NGO in Ghana and are looking for grant funding, USAID and Feed the Future are not currently a viable route. Do not build a funding strategy around them. The routes below are where agribusinesses can look instead. Confirm that a call is open before you spend time or money on an application.

Where Ghanaian agribusinesses should look instead

1. FSRP Matching Grant Scheme (Ministry of Food and Agriculture, World Bank-financed)

The Ministry of Food and Agriculture runs a matching grant scheme under the World Bank-financed West Africa Food System Resilience Programme. Window 1 supports production activities, with a ceiling of US$150,000. Window 2 supports post-production firms such as processors and aggregators with strong smallholder links, with a ceiling of US$500,000. Applicants must be Ghanaian-owned registered companies, and small agribusinesses, SMEs and farmer-based organisations must be registered on the Ghana Agriculture and Agribusiness Platform (GhAAP). Calls are planned yearly and applicants contribute part of the cost, but we could not confirm that a call is open today, so check with FSRP or the Ministry. The earlier IFAD-financed GASIP programme appears to have closed, so do not plan around it.

2. AfDB — Agriculture Fast Track (AFT) Fund

The African Development Bank's AFT Fund is a multi-donor trust fund, approved in 2013, that pays for project preparation rather than construction or running costs: feasibility studies, market research, financial modelling and environmental and social assessments for larger agriculture projects. Earlier calls described grants of roughly US$100,000 to US$1.5 million for projects with total investment above US$1 million. The most recent round we found was announced in 2019 and included Ghana. We could not confirm that a call is open today, so check with the AfDB before you spend time on an application. See our guide to the fund.

3. GIZ — Agri-Business Facility for Africa (ABF)

The Agri-Business Facility for Africa (ABF) ran a Matching Grant Fund for private-sector projects in cashew, cocoa, maize and livestock value chains, where applicants contribute part of the cost. Its latest call, which opened in July 2024, is now shown as closed. ABF's current opportunity is a competition for young and emerging agripreneurs, and its window closed on 13 November 2026. Watch the ABF funding page for a new matching grant call, and check that Ghana and your value chain are eligible before you apply.

"The funding landscape has genuinely shifted. Agribusinesses that built plans around USAID need to redirect that effort, and to check that any call they target is actually open."

What this means if you were planning a USAID application

  • Do not submit an unsolicited USAID or Feed the Future application — there is no active window to receive it
  • Redirect any USAID-specific preparation work — much of the documentation (financial statements, organisational registration, beneficiary data) is reusable for FSRP, bank and other funding applications
  • Reassess your target value chain and geography against the funders above, since eligibility rules differ meaningfully between them
